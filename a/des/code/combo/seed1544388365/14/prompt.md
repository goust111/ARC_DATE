Build nested square frames from a stack of horizontal color stripes.

- Bottom stripe becomes the center solid square with side length = stripe height.
- Each subsequent stripe wraps the current shape with a square frame of thickness = stripe height, in that stripe's color.

<description>
Rotate each blue-started chain of bottom-aligned vertical bars into horizontal bars resting on the same baseline.
Input structure
- The grid has a background and bottom-aligned, one-cell-wide vertical bars.
- Blue bars are markers; red bars are body bars that may be chained after a marker.
Rule
- Starting at each blue bar, repeatedly include the next red bar whose column is exactly the current bar's column plus the current bar's height.
- Clear every included vertical bar, then redraw each included bar horizontally to the right from its original bottom cell, with length equal to its vertical height.
- Red bars that are not reached from a blue marker remain unchanged.
Key insight
- A vertical bar's height is also the width it occupies after rotation, so the chain follows immediate horizontal contact after each rotated bar.
</description>

<reference_puzzle index="3" source="train" puzzle_id="f1bcbc2c" lines="40-55">
<description>
Paint the top-connected middle channel between two orange path components teal.

Input structure
- The background is black.
- One foreground color forms exactly two 4-connected one-pixel-wide path components.
- Optional non-background control pixels may sit in the channel between the two paths.

Rule
- Dilate each path component by one cell in the 8-neighborhood and intersect the two dilations; this gives the one-cell-wide middle channel.
- A control pixel in that channel is a blocker only when its 4-neighbors touch fewer than both path components; otherwise it is part of the channel and is repainted.
- After removing blockers, paint the channel component connected to the top edge teal and keep all other pixels unchanged.

Key insight
- The desired pixels are not found by region filling; they are the geometric overlap of the two paths' one-cell halos, with weakly attached control pixels splitting the channel.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<description>
Advance short marker bars away from the full edge bar, turning completed two-pixel markers into edge-color marks at the opposite side.

Input structure
- The background is the dominant color.
- One non-background color forms a complete one-cell-thick bar on exactly one outer edge.
- The other foreground color appears only in the first two cells inward from that bar, lane by lane.

Rule
- In each lane perpendicular to the edge bar, a one-pixel marker adjacent to the bar grows one more cell inward.
- A two-pixel marker in those first two inward cells is cleared back to background.
- The cleared two-pixel marker is replaced by a two-pixel mark of the edge-bar color at the opposite side of the same lane.

Key insight
- The full edge bar defines both the orientation and the direction of the local two-step marker cycle.
</description>

<description>
Project maroon markers into adjacent grey or pink 3x3 squares, erasing grey squares afterward.

Input structure
The grid has a dominant background, solid 3x3 grey and/or pink squares, and maroon marker pixels or segments directly touching one side of exactly one square.
Rule
Grey squares are erased; their adjacent maroon markers stay in place and stamp the nearest edge cell inside the grey square. Pink squares stay pink; their adjacent maroon markers disappear and stamp the corresponding cell on the square's middle row or middle column.
Key insight
The side touched by a marker determines the projection: grey uses the near edge, while pink uses the centerline, preserving the marker's coordinate along that side.

Match sprites to frames by connected-hole count, then nest each sprite inside its frame.

<description>
Slide yellow pixels inside walled regions in the direction dictated by border-legend arrows.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color; yellow pixels sit inside enclosed regions bounded by colored walls.

Rule:
- For each enclosed region, identify its border color C and look up the legend with fill color C.
- The direction from the legend's center dot to its indicator dot gives the slide direction.
- All yellow pixels in that region slide in that direction, stacking against the far wall.

Key insight: Legend fill color links to region border color; the center→indicator offset encodes the movement vector.

<description>
Compact the checkerboard's moving color toward the edge marked by the teal pixel, leaving a maroon swept trail on the marker line.

Input structure
- A blue/black checkerboard with exactly one teal marker replacing a blue pixel on a non-corner edge.
- The marker's checkerboard parity identifies the moving color (blue); the opposite parity is background (black).

Rule
- If the marker is on the left or right edge, compact moving-color pixels in every row toward that side.
- If the marker is on the top or bottom edge, compact moving-color pixels in every column toward that side.
- Preserve the teal marker at its edge cell.
- On the marker's row or column, color cells swept by shifted moving pixels maroon unless the final compacted line occupies them.

Key insight
The marker selects both the compression direction and the checkerboard color class that moves; maroon records the movement trail only along that marked line.
</description>

<description>
Move the repeated boundary color's rectangles one block inward while leaving singleton boundary rectangles in place.

Input structure:
- The background is the most common color.
- Foreground objects are solid monochrome rectangles touching a grid corner.
- Exactly one foreground color appears as multiple separate 4-connected objects; other colors appear once.

Rule:
- Clear the multiple objects of the repeated color from their original positions.
- For each cleared rectangle, translate it away from the row and column border it touches by exactly its height and width.
- Keep singleton-color objects unchanged; overlapping translated rectangles of the same color merge.

Key insight:
- Multiplicity selects the moving color, and each boundary rectangle's touched edges determine its inward diagonal translation.
</description>

<description>
Move every foreground object straight downward by a number of rows equal to its own pixel count.

Input structure:
- The grid has one dominant background color.
- The foreground is made of separated 4-connected monochromatic objects.
- Objects may move partly below the bottom edge of the grid.

Rule:
- Clear the canvas to the background.
- For each foreground object, compute its area in pixels.
- Redraw the object at the same columns and that many rows lower, clipping pixels below the grid.

Key insight:
- The downward displacement is the object's own area; color and shape only identify which pixels move together.

<description>
Draw a polyline by decoding 3x3 glyph instructions read across a divided grid.

Input structure:
- Left of a full-height vertical divider: 3x3 colored glyphs on bg.
- Right of divider: a single gray (5) start pixel.

Rule:
- Remove the divider; shift the gray start into the left canvas.
- Read glyphs in column-major order (left→right, top→bottom).
- Each glyph's shape (pixel area) encodes a segment: area 4 → down 2, area 5 → right 3, area 6 → left 4, area 7 → left 2.
- Append segments sequentially, stepping down one cell between each.

Key insight: Glyph pixel count (area) uniquely determines both segment length and direction — the shape itself is the instruction.

<reference_puzzle index="13" source="train" puzzle_id="1190bc91" lines="36-49">
<description>
Complete a sparse grid by casting occluding diagonal rays from the longest run and filling the remaining diagonal-connected regions from the short bars.

Input structure
- Black-background grid with one unique longest 4-connected foreground object.
- The longest object is a horizontal or vertical multicolor run; all other foreground objects are shorter monochrome bars.

Rule
- On a blank canvas, process the longest run from left to right or top to bottom; each pixel emits four 45-degree rays that stop at the first already-painted pixel.
- Each remaining monochrome bar fills every still-blank pixel reachable from it by diagonal steps.

Key insight
- Earlier pixels in the long run occlude later diagonal rays, and those ray barriers partition the background into diagonal-connected regions for the short bars.
</description>

<description>
Summarize the foreground line objects as ordered horizontal color stripes.

Input structure:
- A dominant background fills a rectangular grid.
- Each non-background color forms one contiguous one-cell-thick line segment.
- The line segments have distinct topmost rows, and there are at most five of
  them.

Rule:
- Sort the foreground colors by the topmost row occupied by their line
  segment.
- Create a 5x3 output filled with the background color.
- Place one solid 1x3 row for each foreground color, preserving that
  top-to-bottom order and padding any unused rows above with background.

Key insight: The exact line length and slope do not matter; only the
vertical order of the colored line segments is encoded in the output stack.

<description>
Complete each partial marker mask by stamping the matching multicolor sprite over it.

Input structure
- A dominant background contains one or more 8-connected multicolor sprites.
- Separate target masks use colors that already occur inside one of those sprites.
- A target mask may contain only one marker pixel or several same-alignment marker pixels.

Rule
- For each multicolor sprite, test every translation that aligns a target pixel with a
  same-color sprite pixel.
- Keep the translations with maximal exact overlap against the existing target pixels,
  rejecting translations that conflict with other foreground or overlap a source sprite.
- Stamp the whole sprite at those translations; if a non-marker sprite color occurs as
  a single source pixel, consume that source-only marker back to background.

Key insight
The scattered target pixels are partial copies: their same-color overlap with a sprite
determines where the full sprite must be completed.

<description>
Repair markers embedded in the main object and copy the remaining markers one pixel toward the solid edge.

Input structure:
- A mostly background grid contains one solid non-background edge line.
- Apart from that edge and the background, there is one large main object
  color and a smaller marker color.
- Each marker is either embedded in the main object, touching it on at
  least three 4-neighbor sides, or isolated on the background.

- Embedded markers are recolored to the main object color.
- Isolated background markers remain in place and also paint the adjacent
  background pixel in the direction of the solid edge line.

markers extend, while markers surrounded by the main object are simply

<reference_puzzle index="19" source="eval" puzzle_id="e12f9a14" lines="138-152">
<description>
Emit diagonal beams from framed 2x2 blocks through frame gaps; beams interact on collision.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color. Wall color (e.g. green) forms 4x4 frames around 2x2 monochrome blocks.
- Each frame has one or more gap pixels where the wall is missing.

Rule:
- Each gap emits a beam of the inner block's color, traveling diagonally outward (direction set by gap position relative to the block).
- Beams paint every cell they cross until they hit the grid edge or collide.
- 90° collision: both beams turn 45° toward their bisector direction; the intersection cell is left blank (1-pixel gap).
- 45° collision: beams merge into a zigzag (alternating cardinal + diagonal steps) with no gap at the meeting point.

Key insight: Two distinct collision rules — 90° produces a gap and single-pixel diagonal lines; 45° produces no gap and thicker zigzag bands.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

注意事项：
1. 任务不应依赖大量先验知识或复杂计算，而应依赖对模式、结构与空间关系的直觉推理。
2. 关于"计算"的边界：
   - **允许使用**（这些都是人类能心算的视觉量）：数像素 / 孔洞 / 对象 / 邻居（≤10 量级）、比大小、按某个可见属性排序、奇偶判断、取最大 / 最小、用一个计数值作为另一个等量几何量（如 N 个像素 → 长度 N 的射线、面积 N → 边长 N）、判断接触 / 相邻 / 包含、粗略中心位置（簇的视觉中点）、"距离"作为"几步"而不是公式等人类能够直观感受计算的内容。
   - **不建议使用**（这些是算法量而非视觉量，会迫使解题者依赖公式而非视觉直觉）：曼哈顿 / 欧氏 / Chebyshev 距离作为精确查表键、凸包面积、二进制位编码 / 位运算、模运算 / 余数、拓扑距离（BFS / 最短路 / 跳数）、精确几何质心坐标、凸角 / 凹角计数等复杂且不易被看出来的计算。
3. 问题必须能从少量示例中推断出来（通常为 3 对训练样例 + 1 个测试样例）。设计时从人类解题者的视觉角度思考：规则涉及的所有关键要素（对象、关系、属性）必须在网格上肉眼可见，不应依赖人眼无法直接观察到的隐含信息（例如颜色id是不可用的）或需要推算的隐藏属性。规则背后的逻辑本身可以有一定挑战性，但解题所需的所有线索必须能从网格中直接获取。
4. 生成的问题描述旨在促进后续用于创建题目网格的代码生成。因此，对输入 / 输出网格的描述以及规则总结必须极其详细；语言风格应易于理解；对于特定对象（如箭头），必须在描述中明确其绘制方式。

6. 5 个新题的核心推理机制应明显不同——即从观察样例到归纳规则的认知路径不同，而不仅仅是场景、对象或颜色不同。
7. 题目难度应来自机制的隐蔽性而非步骤数。用参考题作为难度标尺——如果你生成的题一个样例就能猜到规则，说明机制太透明，需要加深隐蔽性。
8. 优先从参考题的机制出发进行重组或推演，而非从物理隐喻出发。物理隐喻容易导致大量题目归结为一种模式，例如"沿方向移动到阻挡处停止"。
9. 题目的难度优先来自规则的**结构层数**，而不是单一步骤的复杂度。关于"结构层数"的判定标准：**只有解题者需要主动推理才能完成的步骤才算一层**。肉眼一扫即可获得的信息（如"找到缺口在哪一侧"、"数出像素个数"、"识别哪个对象最大"）不构成独立的推理层——它们只是变换的前置输入，不增加认知难度。真正的多层结构是指每一层都需要解题者发现一个非显然的规律。以两层结构为例：
   - ✗ 伪两层："找到缺口方向"→"把东西推到对面" — 第一步肉眼可见，实际只有一层推理。
   - ✓ 真两层："通过孔洞数量匹配颜色图例"→"用匹配到的颜色重绘对象" — 两步都需要非平凡的归纳。

   如果你发现自己写出的题只是"对每个对象，根据它的某个属性做某变换"的单步规则，请引入一个需要主动推理的前置步骤。**用堆叠 tie-break 规则或复杂数学公式来"加难度"是劣解；用多一层需要归纳的结构嵌套来"加难度"才是好解**。
10. 在符合ARC题目和提示词要求的基础上，鼓励尝试创新规则为多层结构的题目，不局限于两层，可以创新三层或四层结构的题目，但不可为了多结构层数违背要求。

1. 原创性
  新题不可与上述参考题相同或类似，不可只是替换颜色、方向、对象类型等表层要素。判断标准：新题的核心推理路径应与任何一道参考题不同。
2. 确定性
  规则完全确定，同一输入只能产生唯一输出，每一步操作都有明确定义。
3. 可实现性

4. ARC 合规性
  网格尺寸合理（≤30×30），使用颜色不超过 10 种，规则复杂度适中，人类能从 3-5 个样例中发现规律。
