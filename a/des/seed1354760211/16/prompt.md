<description>
Move a triangular sprite across the grid, consuming aligned marker pixels.

Input structure:
- Frames concatenated horizontally with solid divider columns; bg is most common color.
- Each frame: a triangular arrow-shaped sprite and scattered single-pixel markers, all one color.

Rule:
- Start from the rightmost frame.
- At each step, exactly one cardinal direction from the head has reachable
  markers (on a half-line: same row or column); the sprite advances to the
  nearest one.
- On reaching a marker, consume it; sprite re-faces that direction.
- Repeat until no markers remain; output the final sprite position on a clean bg.

Key insight: The triangle's tip encodes facing direction, which sets marker-consumption priority.
</description>

<reference_puzzle index="2" source="train" puzzle_id="db615bd4" lines="111-130">
<description>
Thicken the sparse frame and stamp the external sparse sprites into it in positional order.

Input structure
The grid has a dominant background color, a regular lattice color, one large
sparse rectangular frame, and one or more external monochrome sprites whose
pixels also sit on a two-step lattice.

Rule
Use the largest sparse rectangular perimeter as the frame. Clear that frame
region and the external sprite pixels to background, draw a solid one-pixel
border in the frame color, solidify each sparse sprite into its contiguous
two-step mask, and place the sprites inside the frame with equal gaps.
Wide frames receive sprites left-to-right by source position; tall frames
receive them top-to-bottom by source position.

Key insight
The frame supplies ordered slots: the sprite-to-slot assignment is by visible
source order, not by numeric color or by matching individual cells.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<description>
Inflate a frame's hole-layout and fill each hole with its matching piece.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color. Largest component is a monochrome frame enclosing bg holes.
- Outside pieces composed of k×k blocks; k = side of smallest solid square piece.

Rule:
- Extract connected hole regions inside the frame as slots.
- Match each piece to a slot by comparing block-level shapes under rotation/flip.
- Output the layout scaled by k, with pieces placed in slots; frame color as background.

Key insight: Pieces are k×k-block-scaled versions of holes; downsampling reveals shape matches under all 8 orientations.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="4" source="eval" puzzle_id="7c66cb00" lines="9-24">
<description>
Dock two-tone sprites into color-matched horizontal bars with a color swap.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color.
- Top area: small two-tone sprites (outer color + inner color).
- Below: horizontal bars spanning most of the row, each with a fill color and border color on edges.

Rule:
- Match each sprite to the bar whose fill color equals the sprite's outer color.
- Place the sprite at its original x-position, aligned to the bar's bottom.
- Recolor: sprite outer → bar border, sprite inner → bar fill.
- Erase original sprites from the top area.

Key insight: Color matching is inverted — the sprite's dominant color identifies its target bar, then gets swapped to the bar's accent color.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="5" source="eval" puzzle_id="36a08778" lines="8-21">
<description>
Simulate water falling from top-edge sources, pooling on shelves, and cascading off edges.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color; water = rarest non-bg; shelf = other non-bg.
- Sources: water clusters touching top row. Shelves: horizontal segments.

Rule:
- Water falls down from sources, painting bg cells as water.
- On hitting a shelf, it spreads across the shelf span plus one cell past each end.
- From those overflow positions, water resumes falling independently.

Key insight: Each shelf splits a stream into two at its edges, creating a branching waterfall tree.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="6" source="train" puzzle_id="668eec9a" lines="29-47">
<description>
Summarize the foreground line objects as ordered horizontal color stripes.

Input structure:
- A dominant background fills a rectangular grid.
- Each non-background color forms one contiguous one-cell-thick line segment.
- The line segments have distinct topmost rows, and there are at most five of
  them.

Rule:
- Sort the foreground colors by the topmost row occupied by their line
  segment.
- Create a 5x3 output filled with the background color.
- Place one solid 1x3 row for each foreground color, preserving that
  top-to-bottom order and padding any unused rows above with background.

Key insight: The exact line length and slope do not matter; only the
vertical order of the colored line segments is encoded in the output stack.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="7" source="train" puzzle_id="e9fc42f2" lines="26-43">
<description>
Assemble the foreground objects by docking equal-colored boundary markers edge-to-edge.

Input structure
The grid has a dominant background color and several 4-connected multicolor foreground
objects.  One foreground color is the body color; the less frequent foreground colors are
boundary connector markers, and each marker color appears once as a free end or twice as a
matched docking pair.

Rule
Crop each foreground object to its bounding box and translate the objects so that the two
occurrences of every paired connector color touch on their exposed boundary sides.  Preserve
each object's orientation and colors, then return the tight crop around the assembled
foreground on the original background color.

Key insight
The colored marker pixels are ports: matching colors specify which objects must touch, and
the marker's side of its object determines the direction of the translation.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="8" source="train" puzzle_id="22208ba4" lines="12-27">
<description>
Move the repeated boundary color's rectangles one block inward while leaving singleton boundary rectangles in place.

Input structure:
- The background is the most common color.
- Foreground objects are solid monochrome rectangles touching a grid corner.
- Exactly one foreground color appears as multiple separate 4-connected objects; other colors appear once.

Rule:
- Clear the multiple objects of the repeated color from their original positions.
- For each cleared rectangle, translate it away from the row and column border it touches by exactly its height and width.
- Keep singleton-color objects unchanged; overlapping translated rectangles of the same color merge.

Key insight:
- Multiplicity selects the moving color, and each boundary rectangle's touched edges determine its inward diagonal translation.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="9" source="train" puzzle_id="f1bcbc2c" lines="40-55">
<description>
Paint the top-connected middle channel between two orange path components teal.

Input structure
- The background is black.
- One foreground color forms exactly two 4-connected one-pixel-wide path components.
- Optional non-background control pixels may sit in the channel between the two paths.

Rule
- Dilate each path component by one cell in the 8-neighborhood and intersect the two dilations; this gives the one-cell-wide middle channel.
- A control pixel in that channel is a blocker only when its 4-neighbors touch fewer than both path components; otherwise it is part of the channel and is repainted.
- After removing blockers, paint the channel component connected to the top edge teal and keep all other pixels unchanged.

Key insight
- The desired pixels are not found by region filling; they are the geometric overlap of the two paths' one-cell halos, with weakly attached control pixels splitting the channel.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="10" source="train" puzzle_id="342ae2ed" lines="34-49">
<description>
Draw the missing diagonal connector segment between each pair of same-color rectangular objects.

Input structure:
- The grid has one dominant background color.
- Every foreground color appears as exactly two separated solid rectangular 4-connected objects.
- The two rectangles of each color are diagonally offset with facing corners on a 45-degree line.

Rule:
- Keep all existing rectangles unchanged.
- For each foreground color, connect the lower facing corner of the upper rectangle to the upper facing corner of the lower rectangle.
- Paint only the intervening background pixels on that diagonal, excluding the two endpoint pixels already in the rectangles.

Key insight:
- Same-color rectangles define the endpoints; the output completes the open diagonal between their nearest facing corners.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="11" source="train" puzzle_id="6165ea8f" lines="74-90">
<description>
Build a color-labeled shape-equivalence table from the objects listed on the right edge.

Input structure:
- The background contains one main 4-connected object for each foreground color.
- The rightmost column has one singleton pixel of each foreground color, listed top-to-bottom.
- Those singleton pixels define the row and column order of the output table.

Rule:
- Normalize each main object to its tight mask and compare masks up to rotation or reflection.
- Draw 2x2 color labels across the top and down the left, separated by one background row/column.
- For each off-diagonal table cell, paint a 2x2 red block when the two labeled objects have the same shape under rotation/reflection, otherwise paint a 2x2 grey block.
- Leave diagonal cells and all separators as background.

Key insight:
- Object positions and original orientations are distractors; the relation is dihedral shape equivalence, while the right-edge legend fixes the table order.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<description>
Compress each colored ray pixel inward until it touches that color's 3x3 anchor block.
Input structure
- A uniform background grid with non-background colors.
- Each foreground color contains one solid 3x3 anchor block; all other pixels of that color lie on straight 8-neighbor rays aimed at that block.
Rule
- Keep anchor blocks fixed, erase loose foreground pixels, and for each ray pack its pixels into consecutive cells adjacent to the anchor along the same ray.

<reference_puzzle index="13" source="train" puzzle_id="652646ff" lines="84-105">
<description>
Recover each partially hidden 6x6 ring sprite and stack complete copies from top layer to bottom layer.

Input structure:
- The dominant color is the background.
- Several non-background colors are visible fragments of the same 12-pixel
  outline ring in a 6x6 box; small off-template marker pixels are noise.
- Ring fragments overlap, so upper rings replace some expected pixels of
  lower rings, and a ring may also be clipped by the input boundary.

Rule:
- Identify every substantial color whose pixels all lie on one translated
  copy of the ring template.
- For each missing in-bounds template pixel, the color currently occupying
  that pixel is above the hidden ring.
- Topologically sort these occlusion relations from top to bottom, then draw
  one complete 6x6 ring for each color in that order on a background canvas.

Key insight:
- The output is not a crop of the scene; it is the layer stack of complete
  ring sprites reconstructed from their visible fragments.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="15" source="eval" puzzle_id="581f7754" lines="8-22">
<description>
Slide multi-color sprites to align their marker pixels with matching isolated border anchors.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color.
- Multi-color sprites in the interior; each has a minority-color "marker" pixel.
- Isolated border pixels (non-bg with all 4-neighbors bg) act as anchors.

Rule:
- Match each sprite to the border anchor sharing its marker color.
- Top/bottom-edge anchor (non-corner): slide sprite horizontally so marker column = anchor column.
- Left/right-edge anchor (or corner): slide sprite vertically so marker row = anchor row.

Key insight: The anchor's edge position determines the slide axis — top/bottom means horizontal, left/right (or corner) means vertical.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="16" source="eval" puzzle_id="d8e07eb2" lines="29-47">
<description>
Look up query glyphs in a reference table and mark whether they form a line.

Input structure:
- Teal background with two full-width pink separator rows.
- Above the first separator is a query row of 5x5 glyph cells.
- Between separators is a reference table of the same 5x5 cells; a glyph is
  identified by both its color and its 3x3 shape.

Rule:
- Green-fill the background inside every table cell whose glyph exactly
  appears in the query row.
- If the matched table cells form one complete row or one complete column,
  also green-fill the query band and the bottom verdict band.
- Otherwise leave the query band unchanged and fill the bottom verdict band red.

Key insight: The bottom color is not a count; it is the yes/no answer to
whether the queried glyphs align as a full row or column in the table.
</description>

<reference_puzzle index="17" source="train" puzzle_id="84551f4c" lines="42-53">
<description>
Rotate each blue-started chain of bottom-aligned vertical bars into horizontal bars resting on the same baseline.
Input structure
- The grid has a background and bottom-aligned, one-cell-wide vertical bars.
- Blue bars are markers; red bars are body bars that may be chained after a marker.
Rule
- Starting at each blue bar, repeatedly include the next red bar whose column is exactly the current bar's column plus the current bar's height.
- Clear every included vertical bar, then redraw each included bar horizontally to the right from its original bottom cell, with length equal to its vertical height.
- Red bars that are not reached from a blue marker remain unchanged.
Key insight
- A vertical bar's height is also the width it occupies after rotation, so the chain follows immediate horizontal contact after each rotated bar.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<description>
Slide yellow pixels inside walled regions in the direction dictated by border-legend arrows.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color; yellow pixels sit inside enclosed regions bounded by colored walls.
- Border legends: small rectangles on the grid edge whose fill color C matches a region's border color. Each legend contains a center dot and an indicator dot encoding a direction.

Rule:
- For each enclosed region, identify its border color C and look up the legend with fill color C.
- The direction from the legend's center dot to its indicator dot gives the slide direction.
- All yellow pixels in that region slide in that direction, stacking against the far wall.

Key insight: Legend fill color links to region border color; the center→indicator offset encodes the movement vector.
</description>

<description>
Recolor every pixel whose color is not one of the two most frequent input colors to orange.

Input structure:
- The grid contains at least three colors.
- Orange is absent from the input and is used only as the output replacement color.
- Exactly two colors occupy the top two frequency ranks; they may tie with each

Rule:
- Preserve every pixel whose color is one of the two most frequent input colors.
- Replace every other pixel with orange.

Key insight:
- The visible distinction is not the numeric identity of the colors but their

  into the new orange color.

注意事项：
1. 任务不应依赖大量先验知识或复杂计算，而应依赖对模式、结构与空间关系的直觉推理。
2. 若涉及计算，只允许使用简单的计数、排序与数量比较；不允许使用复杂的数学公式或运算。
3. 问题必须能从少量示例中推断出来，但不代表题目一定简单。设计时需从人类解题者的视觉角度思考：规则涉及的所有关键要素（对象、关系、属性）必须在网格上肉眼可见，不应依赖人眼无法直接观察到的隐含信息或需要推算的隐藏属性。规则可以很难发现，难到反复对比所有样例仍需要深度思考才能归纳出来，但一旦发现后必须能用来解释所有样例且产出唯一确定的输出。
4. 生成的问题描述旨在促进后续用于创建题目网格的代码生成。因此，对输入/输出网格的描述以及规则总结必须极其详细；语言风格应易于理解；对于特定对象（如箭头），必须在描述中明确其绘制方式。
5. 描述谜题时避免使用 0 或 1 这类颜色编号；应使用颜色名称，例如 “red”。可用颜色如下：Black、Blue、Red、Green、Yellow、Grey、Magenta、Orange、Azure、Maroon。
6. 五个新题的核心推理机制应明显不同——即从观察样例到归纳规则的认知路径不同，而不仅仅是场景、对象或颜色不同。
7.  题目难度应来自机制的隐蔽性而非步骤数。关键信息难以准确定位，需要反复对比尝试不同的角度才能发现真正的规则。


生成后对生成的题执行以下四项审查（审查内容不需要保存到文件）：
1. 原创性
  新题不可与上述20道参考题相同或者是类似，不可只是替换了颜色、方向、对象类型、维度等表层要素。判断标准：新题的核心推理路径（解题者从样例中归纳规则的认知过程）应与任何一道参考题不同。
2. 确定性
  规则完全确定，同一输入只能产生唯一输出，每一步操作都有明确定义。
3. 可实现性
  所有必要细节都已给出——对象如何生成、如何识别、如何变换、边界情况如何处理，读完描述后可以直接写代码。
4. ARC 合规性
  网格尺寸合理（≤30×30），使用颜色不超过 10 种，规则复杂度适中，人类能从 3-5个样例中发现规律。
