<description>
Paint the top-connected middle channel between two orange path components teal.

Input structure
- The background is black.
- One foreground color forms exactly two 4-connected one-pixel-wide path components.
- Optional non-background control pixels may sit in the channel between the two paths.

Rule
- Dilate each path component by one cell in the 8-neighborhood and intersect the two dilations; this gives the one-cell-wide middle channel.
- A control pixel in that channel is a blocker only when its 4-neighbors touch fewer than both path components; otherwise it is part of the channel and is repainted.
- After removing blockers, paint the channel component connected to the top edge teal and keep all other pixels unchanged.

Key insight
- The desired pixels are not found by region filling; they are the geometric overlap of the two paths' one-cell halos, with weakly attached control pixels splitting the channel.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<description>
Expand the colored runs from the left reference panel into the blank right panel.

Input structure:
- A uniform rectangular frame surrounds the grid.
- One full-height divider of the frame color splits a left reference panel
  from a blank right panel.
- Both panels have one background margin column on each side of their active
  columns; the left active columns contain two-column endpoint slots, possibly
  connected by same-color horizontal runs.

Rule:
- Keep the original grid and paint only in the right panel's active columns.
- A source run exactly matching the left endpoint slot is copied to the left
  two columns of the right active span.
- A source run exactly matching the right endpoint slot is copied to the right
  two columns of the right active span.
- A source run connecting the endpoint slots is stretched across the entire
  right active span.

Key insight:
The left panel is a compressed legend for the right panel: endpoint markers
stay two pixels wide, while connectors between endpoints expand to fill the
distance between them.

<reference_puzzle index="4" source="eval" puzzle_id="64efde09" lines="68-83">
<description>
Copy colored control-ray ports from one rectangular sprite orientation to every matching transformed sprite.

Input structure:
- bg is the most common color.
- Several dense 2-by-N or N-by-2 rectangular sprites are dihedral transforms of one template.
- Isolated single-pixel control markers sit on rays projected outward from the dominant long edge of some sprites.

Rule:
- For each sprite, the active edge is the long side whose cells have the strongest same-color majority.
- A control marker seen first along an outward ray from an active-edge cell assigns its color to that template cell.
- On every transformed sprite, draw that assigned color outward from the corresponding active-edge cell through bg cells until blocked or out of bounds.

Key insight:
- The marker positions are not absolute; they label cells of a shared rectangular template, then the labels follow each sprite's rotation/reflection.
</description>

<description>
Assemble the foreground objects by docking equal-colored boundary markers edge-to-edge.

Input structure
The grid has a dominant background color and several 4-connected multicolor foreground
objects.  One foreground color is the body color; the less frequent foreground colors are
boundary connector markers, and each marker color appears once as a free end or twice as a
matched docking pair.

Rule
Crop each foreground object to its bounding box and translate the objects so that the two
occurrences of every paired connector color touch on their exposed boundary sides.  Preserve
each object's orientation and colors, then return the tight crop around the assembled
foreground on the original background color.

Key insight
The colored marker pixels are ports: matching colors specify which objects must touch, and
the marker's side of its object determines the direction of the translation.

<description>
Crop a board cluster and rotate its color layers based on outside cue sprites.

Rule:
- Tight-crop around the board cluster.
- For each color layer inside the crop, find its same-color cue outside.

Key insight: Cue sprite size mod 4 encodes rotation amount for its matching color layer.

<description>
Keep the largest 8-connected motif and replace the corner/control pixels with a solid corner triangle.

Input structure
- The background is the dominant color.
- Exactly one grid corner contains a foreground control pixel; it gives the
  corner from which the triangle is painted.
- There is one unique largest 8-connected monochrome foreground object, the
  motif that remains in the output; all other foreground pixels are controls.
- Every foreground component outside that motif is a singleton control pixel.
- The fill color is the unique control color that appears in at least two
  non-corner control pixels.

Rule
- Start from a blank background canvas and redraw only the largest motif.
- Count the non-corner control pixels; that count is the triangle side.
- Paint the fill color on the right-triangle region where those two distances
  from the marked corner's edges sum to less than the side length.

Key insight
- The scattered singleton controls are counted into a compact triangular
  stack at the marked corner; only the largest motif is not part of the count.
</description>

<description>
Move each mirrored bottom source pixel to the far end of its trail and redraw the top half as its reflection.

Input structure
- A solid non-background row separates equal-height top and bottom halves.
- The top half has one marker color on the background.
- In the bottom half, exactly one color has positions that mirror the top markers across the separator, and the other color forms trails touching those source pixels.

Rule
- Identify the bottom source color by reflecting each bottom color into the top half and matching the top marker set.
- For every bottom source pixel, follow its unique 4-connected trail of the other bottom color to the trail endpoint.
- Clear the old top markers, bottom sources, and trails; place the source color at each endpoint and place the top marker color at the reflected endpoint.

Key insight
- The top half is only a mirror display of the bottom moving sources; the actual motion is encoded by the trail attached to each bottom source pixel.

Inflate a frame's hole-layout and fill each hole with its matching piece.

Rule:

<reference_puzzle index="11" source="train" puzzle_id="d6542281" lines="72-91">
<description>
Complete each partial marker mask by stamping the matching multicolor sprite over it.

Input structure
- A dominant background contains one or more 8-connected multicolor sprites.
- Separate target masks use colors that already occur inside one of those sprites.
- A target mask may contain only one marker pixel or several same-alignment marker pixels.

Rule
- For each multicolor sprite, test every translation that aligns a target pixel with a
  same-color sprite pixel.
- Keep the translations with maximal exact overlap against the existing target pixels,
  rejecting translations that conflict with other foreground or overlap a source sprite.
- Stamp the whole sprite at those translations; if a non-marker sprite color occurs as
  a single source pixel, consume that source-only marker back to background.

Key insight
The scattered target pixels are partial copies: their same-color overlap with a sprite
determines where the full sprite must be completed.
</description>

<reference_puzzle index="12" source="train" puzzle_id="f8f52ecc" lines="107-122">
<description>
Connect same-colored foreground components with one-cell-wide orthogonal paths around teal wall segments.

Input structure
- The dominant color is the background.
- Teal cells are fixed wall segments and are never source connectors.
- Other foreground colors appear as one or more 4-connected components; colors with a single component stay unchanged.

Rule
- For each non-background, non-teal color, read its 4-connected components in row-major order.
- Connect each consecutive pair by the shortest clear L-shaped path between the two components.
- Clear paths may pass only through background or the same color, and they preserve the previous travel axis when both bends are possible.

Key insight
- The output is not a global recolor: each color traces a routed chain through its own components while teal cells and other colors act as blockers.
</description>

<description>
Replicate a motif across quadrants with scaling and reflection keyed to red anchor blocks.

Input structure:
- Grid divided into four quadrants by a full-row and full-column divider of a single non-bg color.
- Each quadrant contains a red (2) square anchor block of varying size.
- The top-left quadrant also has a colored motif attached to its red anchor.

Rule:
- Use the top-left quadrant's motif as the source pattern.
- For each other quadrant, scale the motif by the ratio of that quadrant's red block size to the source red block size.
- Reflect horizontally for right-side quadrants, vertically for bottom quadrants (both for bottom-right).
- Align the scaled/reflected motif so its red region overlaps the target anchor, then stamp non-bg pixels.

Key insight: Red block size encodes the scale factor; quadrant position encodes the reflection axes.
</description>

<description>
Swap every sprite to the opposite prototype while preserving scale and position.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color. Two distinct small sprite patterns (prototypes) appear at various scales.
- Each sprite instance is a scaled copy of one of the two prototypes, placed on the grid.

Rule:
- Identify the two prototype patterns from the smallest-area sprites.
- For each sprite, determine which prototype it matches and its integer scale factor.
- Replace it with the other prototype at the same scale, aligned by an internal anchor region (color-1 pixels).

Key insight: Every sprite toggles to the alternate prototype; the anchor sub-region inside each sprite pins the alignment so swapped sprites land in the correct position regardless of scale.

注意事项：
1. 任务不应依赖大量先验知识或复杂计算，而应依赖对模式、结构与空间关系的直觉推理。
2. 关于"计算"的边界：
   - **允许使用**（这些都是人类能心算的视觉量）：数像素 / 孔洞 / 对象 / 邻居（≤10 量级）、比大小、按某个可见属性排序、奇偶判断、取最大 / 最小、用一个计数值作为另一个等量几何量（如 N 个像素 → 长度 N 的射线、面积 N → 边长 N）、判断接触 / 相邻 / 包含、粗略中心位置（簇的视觉中点）、"距离"作为"几步"而不是公式等人类能够直观感受计算的内容。
   - **禁止使用**（这些是算法量而非视觉量，会迫使解题者依赖公式而非视觉直觉）：曼哈顿 / 欧氏 / Chebyshev 距离作为精确查表键、凸包面积、二进制位编码 / 位运算、模运算 / 余数、拓扑距离（BFS / 最短路 / 跳数）、精确几何质心坐标、凸角 / 凹角计数等复杂且不易被看出来的计算都是禁止使用的。
3. 问题必须能从少量示例中推断出来（通常为 3 对训练样例 + 1 个测试样例）。设计时从人类解题者的视觉角度思考：规则涉及的所有关键要素（对象、关系、属性）必须在网格上肉眼可见，不应依赖人眼无法直接观察到的隐含信息（例如颜色id和颜色亮度是禁止使用的）或需要推算的隐藏属性。规则背后的逻辑本身可以有一定挑战性，但解题所需的所有线索必须能从网格中直接获取。
4. 生成的问题描述旨在促进后续用于创建题目网格的代码生成。因此，对输入 / 输出网格的描述以及规则总结必须极其详细；语言风格应易于理解；对于特定对象（如箭头），必须在描述中明确其绘制方式。
5. 描述谜题时避免使用 0 或 1 这类颜色编号；应使用颜色名称，例如 "red"。可用颜色如下：Black、Blue、Red、Green、Yellow、Grey、Magenta、Orange、Azure、Maroon。
6. 5 个新题的核心推理机制应明显不同——即从观察样例到归纳规则的认知路径不同，而不仅仅是场景、对象或颜色不同。
7. 题目难度应来自机制的隐蔽性而非步骤数。用参考题作为难度标尺——如果你生成的题一个样例就能猜到规则，说明机制太透明，需要加深隐蔽性。
8. 优先从参考题的机制出发进行重组或推演，而非从物理隐喻出发。物理隐喻容易导致大量题目归结为一种模式，例如"沿方向移动到阻挡处停止"。
9. 题目的难度优先来自规则的**结构层数**，而不是单一步骤的复杂度。关于"结构层数"的判定标准：**只有解题者需要主动推理才能完成的步骤才算一层**。肉眼一扫即可获得的信息（如"找到缺口在哪一侧"、"数出像素个数"、"识别哪个对象最大"）不构成独立的推理层——它们只是变换的前置输入，不增加认知难度。真正的多层结构是指每一层都需要解题者发现一个非显然的规律。以两层结构为例：
   - ✗ 伪两层："找到缺口方向"→"把东西推到对面" — 第一步肉眼可见，实际只有一层推理。
   - ✓ 真两层："通过孔洞数量匹配颜色图例"→"用匹配到的颜色重绘对象" — 两步都需要非平凡的归纳。

   如果你发现自己写出的题只是"对每个对象，根据它的某个属性做某变换"的单步规则，请引入一个需要主动推理的前置步骤。**用堆叠 tie-break 规则或复杂数学公式来"加难度"是劣解；用多一层需要归纳的结构嵌套来"加难度"才是好解**。
10. 在符合ARC题目和提示词要求的基础上，鼓励尝试创新规则为多层结构的题目，不局限于两层，可以创新三层或四层结构的题目，但不可为了多结构层数违背要求。

生成后对生成的题执行以下四项审查（审查内容不需要保存到文件）：
1. 原创性
  新题不可与上述参考题相同或类似，不可只是替换颜色、方向、对象类型等表层要素。判断标准：新题的核心推理路径应与任何一道参考题不同。
2. 确定性
  规则完全确定，同一输入只能产生唯一输出，每一步操作都有明确定义。
3. 可实现性
  所有必要细节都已给出——对象如何生成、如何识别、如何变换、边界情况如何处理，读完描述后可以直接写代码。
4. ARC 合规性
  网格尺寸合理（≤30×30），使用颜色不超过 10 种，规则复杂度适中，人类能从 3-5 个样例中发现规律。没有使用上述注意事项第二条禁止使用的复杂计算。
