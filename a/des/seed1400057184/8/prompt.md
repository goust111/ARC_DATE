<description>
Mark the missing center of each plus-shaped arrangement of four two-pixel bars with yellow.

Input structure:
- The background is the dominant color.
- The single foreground color forms isolated 4-connected objects, each a horizontal or vertical two-pixel bar.
- Some bars form an implied plus sign: two horizontal bars and two vertical bars around an empty center, with a one-pixel gap between the center and each bar.

Rule:
- Keep the input unchanged except at implied plus-sign centers.
- For every background pixel with foreground bars at offsets 2-3 cells left, right, above, and below, recolor that center yellow.
- The one-pixel gaps immediately next to the center in the four cardinal directions must remain background.

Key insight:
- The yellow pixels are not extending a line; they identify the shared missing center where four separated two-pixel bars align as a plus sign.

<reference_puzzle index="2" source="train" puzzle_id="1b8318e3" lines="86-105">
<description>
Move each isolated colored pixel into the one-cell frame around a grey 2x2 block.

Input structure:
- Background is the dominant color.
- Grey foreground objects are solid 2x2 anchor blocks.
- Every other non-background, non-grey cell is a colored singleton source.

Rule:
- Keep the grey anchors fixed and clear all colored singleton source pixels.
- For each singleton and each anchor, the normal destination is the cell reached by
  clamping the pixel into the anchor's one-cell outer frame.
- If a diagonally outside singleton has an exact 8-direction sightline into an anchor,
  also allow the cell just before the first grey pixel on that ray.
- Choose distinct destinations that minimize total king-move distance; at equal
  distance prefer exact sightlines, then the shorter Manhattan movement.

Key insight: The grey 2x2 blocks define magnetic frames; conflicts are resolved
structurally by nearest-frame assignment rather than by color identity.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="3" source="train" puzzle_id="a6953f00" lines="45-63">
<description>
Extract the top-left or top-right 2x2 corner crop selected by the top edge clue.

Input structure
- The input is a square color grid whose answer is one of the two 2x2 crops
  touching the top edge and a grid corner.
- A repeated color on the top row, when present, acts as the primary side cue.
- Otherwise, the two top corner colors have different whole-grid frequencies.

Rule
- If a top-row color repeats, find the repeated top-row occurrence nearest to
  a left or right edge and take the 2x2 top-corner crop on that side.
- If the top row has no repeated color, compare the whole-grid frequencies of
  the two top-corner colors and take the side whose corner color is rarer.

Key insight
- The task is not asking for an arbitrary 2x2 search: the decisive information
  is on the top edge, and it selects which top corner crop to keep.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="4" source="train" puzzle_id="d968ffd4" lines="15-28">
<description>
Extend each of two aligned rectangular marker objects toward the other, filling half of the gap across the full perpendicular span.

Input structure
- A dominant background contains exactly two solid, same-sized monochromatic rectangles.
- The rectangles share either the same row span or the same column span, with a background gap between their facing edges.

Rule
- If the rectangles are side-by-side, color the left half of the inter-object gap across every row with the left object's color, and the right half with the right object's color.
- If one rectangle is above the other, color the upper half of the gap across every column with the upper object's color, and the lower half with the lower object's color.
- When the gap length is odd, leave the center row/column as background.

Key insight
- The marker objects are endpoints of an axial corridor; their own size identifies the facing edge, while the filled corridor spans the whole grid perpendicular to that axis.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="5" source="eval" puzzle_id="20270e3b" lines="7-21">
<description>
Connect two sprites using matching connector pixels, then tight-crop.

Input structure:
- Background color `bg`, sprite color `obj`, connector color `ctrl` (small, 2 components).
- Two main foreground sprites, plus possibly tiny detached pixels belonging to one sprite.

Rule:
- Identify the **base** sprite (closest to grid top-left).
- Translate the other sprite so its connector sits one cell above the base's connector.
- Stamp the plug onto the base; remove connectors (recolor `ctrl` to `obj`).
- Output is the tight crop around all non-background pixels.

Key insight: The connector pixels define the unique docking point and direction.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="6" source="train" puzzle_id="fc4aaf52" lines="17-34">
<description>
Split the two-color object horizontally, swap its colors, and slide the upper half to the right.

Input structure:
- The grid has one background color and exactly two foreground colors.
- All foreground pixels form one 4-connected object with an even-height bbox.
- The object has foreground pixels on both rows adjacent to its horizontal midpoint.

Rule:
- Split the object into upper and lower halves at the midpoint of its bbox.
- Swap the two foreground colors in both halves.
- Keep the lower half in place and shift the upper half right until its lower-left
  boundary pixel is diagonally adjacent to the lower half's upper-right boundary pixel.

Key insight:
- The object is not copied or reflected; it is broken at the horizontal waist,
  recolored by exchanging the two visible object colors, then rejoined diagonally.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<description>
Transmit colored tokens along wire networks, converting each to its complementary 3×3 pattern.

Input structure:
- bg = background. Non-bg cells form wire networks and 3×3 tokens (solid blocks or X/diagonal patterns) near wire endpoints.

Rule:
- Every solid 3×3 token is replaced in-place by a diamond (center + 4 cardinal neighbors).
- Each token near a wire endpoint sends its complement to the opposite endpoint: solid emits X, X emits diamond.

Key insight: Token shape cycles solid → X → diamond; wires route each complement to the far end.
</description>

<reference_puzzle index="8" source="eval" puzzle_id="67e490f4" lines="8-22">
<description>
Fill holes inside a large template frame using shape-matched outside sprites.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color. One large monochrome frame object (template).
- Inside the frame: holes of various shapes (non-frame-color regions).
- Outside the frame: small monochrome sprites scattered on bg.

Rule:
- Crop to the frame's bounding box; fill entirely with frame color.
- For each hole, find an outside sprite whose shape matches (rotation/flip allowed).
- Fill the hole with the matched sprite's color. If multiple sprites match, use the most frequent color.

Key insight: Sprites act as shape-coded color keys — their shape identifies which hole they fill, their color determines the fill color.
</description>

<reference_puzzle index="9" source="eval" puzzle_id="581f7754" lines="8-22">
<description>
Slide multi-color sprites to align their marker pixels with matching isolated border anchors.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color.
- Multi-color sprites in the interior; each has a minority-color "marker" pixel.
- Isolated border pixels (non-bg with all 4-neighbors bg) act as anchors.

Rule:
- Match each sprite to the border anchor sharing its marker color.
- Top/bottom-edge anchor (non-corner): slide sprite horizontally so marker column = anchor column.
- Left/right-edge anchor (or corner): slide sprite vertically so marker row = anchor row.

Key insight: The anchor's edge position determines the slide axis — top/bottom means horizontal, left/right (or corner) means vertical.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<description>
Convert each hollow 3x3 symbol to one pixel and slide the pixels clockwise from the marked edge.

Input structure
- The grid has a background, one full-edge marker line, and monochrome hollow 3x3 foreground symbols.
- The symbols lie on a coarse row/column lattice; fully empty lattice rows or columns are absent from the output.

Rule
- Replace each hollow 3x3 symbol with one pixel of its color in the corresponding coarse-grid cell.
- The marker edge gives the compaction direction by clockwise border travel: top -> right, right -> down, bottom -> left, left -> up.
- Preserve the order of non-background pixels while sliding them as far as possible in that direction.

Key insight
- The large picture is a scaled symbolic grid, and the blue edge marker acts as a gravity direction rather than as output content.

<description>
Dock train sprites onto station groups by matching coupler count, then orient via divider-color markers.

Input structure:
- bg; colored stripes (edge + internal) partition grid into rectangular regions.
- Color 8 forms train bodies; color 9 marks both isolated stations and train-attached couplers.
- Stripe-colored pixels on trains are orientation markers.

Rule:
- Stations = 8-connected 9-clusters not adjacent to any 8. Trains = components containing 8.
- Match train to station group where |couplers| == |station size|.
- Rotate/flip train so marker pixels face the same-color boundary stripe of the target region.
- Translate so couplers align exactly onto station cells; erase original position.

Key insight: Coupler count selects the docking target; marker color encodes which boundary stripe to face, fixing the unique orientation.

<reference_puzzle index="16" source="train" puzzle_id="7d7772cc" lines="70-92">
<description>
Move each source pixel toward the separator when it matches its aligned reference pixel, otherwise to the far edge.

Input structure:
- A single full separator line is part of a rectangular frame; the frame's
  extra cells mark the reference side.
- On the reference side, one row or column of colored pixels is aligned
  with one row or column of source pixels on the opposite side.
- Each side has its own dominant background color.

Rule:
- Compare each source pixel with the reference pixel on the same row or column.
- If their colors match, place the source pixel in the cell adjacent to the
  separator on the source side.
- If their colors differ, place the source pixel on the far outer edge along
  the same row or column.
- Erase the original source pixels to the source-side background; keep the
  frame and reference pixels unchanged.

Key insight:
The extra frame cells identify which side is the reference; equality with the
aligned reference pixel decides whether each source pixel moves inward or outward.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<description>
Slide each black border rectangle to the opposite border, recoloring its swept stripe orange and leaving the rectangle black at the far edge.

Input structure
- The grid contains black rectangular 4-connected masks, each touching exactly one outer border.
- Grey cells form incidental pattern content; orange is the trail color used when a black mask slides.

Rule
- Treat all black rectangles as moving simultaneously.
- A rectangle touching the top or bottom edge moves vertically to the opposite edge in the same columns.
- A rectangle touching the left or right edge moves horizontally to the opposite edge in the same rows.
- Recolor the complete swept row or column stripe to orange, then stamp every destination rectangle in black.

Key insight
- Edge contact gives the slide direction; the output is the set of opposite-edge black rectangles plus their orange trails.
</description>

<description>
Move the left-edge fragment into its same-colored anchor fragment to complete a solid rectangle.

注意事项：
1. 任务不应依赖大量先验知识或复杂计算，而应依赖对模式、结构与空间关系的直觉推理。
2. 若涉及计算，只允许使用简单的计数、排序与数量比较；不允许使用复杂的数学公式或运算。
3. 问题必须能从少量示例中推断出来，但不代表题目一定简单。设计时需从人类解题者的视觉角度思考：规则涉及的所有关键要素（对象、关系、属性）必须在网格上肉眼可见，不应依赖人眼无法直接观察到的隐含信息或需要推算的隐藏属性。规则可以很难发现，难到反复对比所有样例仍需要深度思考才能归纳出来，但一旦发现后必须能用来解释所有样例且产出唯一确定的输出。
4. 生成的问题描述旨在促进后续用于创建题目网格的代码生成。因此，对输入/输出网格的描述以及规则总结必须极其详细；语言风格应易于理解；对于特定对象（如箭头），必须在描述中明确其绘制方式。
5. 描述谜题时避免使用 0 或 1 这类颜色编号；应使用颜色名称，例如 “red”。可用颜色如下：Black、Blue、Red、Green、Yellow、Grey、Magenta、Orange、Azure、Maroon。
6. 五个新题的核心推理机制应明显不同——即从观察样例到归纳规则的认知路径不同，而不仅仅是场景、对象或颜色不同。
7.  题目难度应来自机制的隐蔽性而非步骤数。关键信息难以准确定位，需要反复对比尝试不同的角度才能发现真正的规则。


生成后对生成的题执行以下四项审查（审查内容不需要保存到文件）：
1. 原创性
  新题不可与上述20道参考题相同或者是类似，不可只是替换了颜色、方向、对象类型、维度等表层要素。判断标准：新题的核心推理路径（解题者从样例中归纳规则的认知过程）应与任何一道参考题不同。
2. 确定性
  规则完全确定，同一输入只能产生唯一输出，每一步操作都有明确定义。
3. 可实现性
  所有必要细节都已给出——对象如何生成、如何识别、如何变换、边界情况如何处理，读完描述后可以直接写代码。
4. ARC 合规性
  网格尺寸合理（≤30×30），使用颜色不超过 10 种，规则复杂度适中，人类能从 3-5个样例中发现规律。
