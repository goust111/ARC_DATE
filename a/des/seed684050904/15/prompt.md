<reference_puzzle index="2" source="train" puzzle_id="bae5c565" lines="10-28">
<description>
Erase the top palette row and redraw it below as a widening wedge centered on its missing cell.

Input structure
- The top row is a palette row with exactly one background gap.
- Below it, the only foreground is one vertical marker bar of a single color.
- The marker bar is either in the gap column or immediately beside it.

Rule
- Clear the grid to background.
- Starting at the top of the marker bar, each next row copies one more
  column of the top-row palette on both sides of the gap.
- Fill the gap column with the marker color, and keep the vertical marker bar.

Key insight
- The lone background cell in the top row is the center of the expanding
  copy; the lower vertical bar provides the center color and the row where
  the expansion begins.
</description>

<reference_puzzle index="3" source="train" puzzle_id="22425bda" lines="58-76">
<description>
List the colors of overlaid straight lines in their only possible bottom-to-top crossing order.

Input structure
The background is the dominant color. Every foreground color is the visible part
of one full one-cell-thick horizontal, vertical, or 45-degree diagonal line.
Higher lines may hide pixels of lower lines at crossings.

Rule
Recover each full line from its visible collinear pixels. At any cell where
multiple recovered lines cross, the visible color is above every other line
through that cell. Return the unique ordering of all line colors that respects
those above/below constraints and whose consecutive colors are lines that
geometrically cross.

Key insight
The visible crossing colors give a partial layer order, and the intended answer
is the unique Hamiltonian chain through the line-intersection graph from the
bottom layer to the top layer.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="4" source="eval" puzzle_id="6ffbe589" lines="83-98">
<description>
Crop a board cluster and rotate its color layers based on outside cue sprites.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color.
- A main multi-color board cluster (largest foreground region).
- Small monochrome cue sprites outside the board for the colors that rotate.

Rule:
- Tight-crop around the board cluster.
- For each color layer inside the crop, find its same-color cue outside.
- Rotate that layer clockwise by (cue pixel count mod 4) × 90°.
- No outside cue → no rotation.

Key insight: Cue sprite size mod 4 encodes rotation amount for its matching color layer.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="5" source="eval" puzzle_id="a32d8b75" lines="262-275">
<description>
Assemble and place enlarged sprites on a canvas using legend-panel instructions.

Input structure:
- Color-6 separators divide the grid into a left legend column and a main canvas.
- Each legend panel has (top to bottom): a unit sprite, a frame template, a 5x5 bordered square with a position-marker pixel, and a 3x3 rotation clock-hand.

Rule:
- Tile the unit sprite into each active cell of the frame to build an enlarged composite.
- Rotate the composite per the clock-hand direction (up=0, right=90, down=180, left=270 degrees).
- Place it on the canvas anchored at the position encoded by the marker pixel's row/col in the bordered square's 3x3 inner grid.

Key insight: The marker pixel's position in a 3x3 grid jointly encodes vertical and horizontal anchor alignment (start/middle/end).
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="6" source="train" puzzle_id="963c33f8" lines="86-108">
<description>
Drop the columns of the top marker downward, stopping blue-ended columns on grey obstacles and letting maroon-ended columns pass to the bottom.

Input structure
- The background is the dominant color.
- The most numerous non-background color forms stationary grey obstacle pixels.
- The other two colors form a single 3x3 top-edge marker whose top two rows
  are maroon and whose bottom row contains maroon or blue control pixels.

Rule
- Erase the top marker.
- Treat each marker column as a vertical drop retaining its three colors.
- A column whose bottom control pixel is blue stops immediately above the
  first grey pixel below that column, or bottom-aligns if none exists.
- Contiguous maroon-ended columns pass through obstacles and bottom-align;
  if such a run has a clear down-right slide from a grey ledge, shift the
  run one column right before bottom-aligning.
- When a maroon-ended drop lands through a grey bar on the bottom edge, clear
  the rest of the marker-width gap in that bottom bar.

Key insight
- The bottom row of the top marker is a control strip: blue cells mean
  "stick to the first obstacle," while maroon cells mean "fall through."
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<reference_puzzle index="7" source="eval" puzzle_id="1ae2feb7" lines="30-45">
<description>
Extend row bars periodically across the opposite side of a separator column.

Input structure:
- bg = background. A near-solid vertical separator divides the grid into two sides.
- One side contains horizontal colored bars; the other side is blank target space.
- Bars may be stacked by row and may sit at different distances from the separator.

Rule:
- Identify the separator as the column whose non-bg cells are one color.
- Treat the denser side as the bar side and collect each contiguous row run.
- Process bars from nearest to farthest from the separator; for each bar, paint its
  color every bar-length cells into the blank side without overwriting earlier paint.

Key insight: Each bar's length is the period for its extension, and nearer bars claim target cells first.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<description>
Extend a green path from one grid edge to the opposite, routing around red bars at a distance dictated by their blue pixel count.

Input structure:
- bg = azure (8). Red/blue bars (color 2/1) act as obstacles.
- A single green (3) pixel sits on a grid border — the path start.

Rule:
- Each bar's keep-out radius equals its number of blue pixels; the path must stay farther than that from every bar cell.
- The green pixel extends inward toward the opposite edge, turning at right angles to navigate around forbidden zones.
- After the path is drawn, all blue pixels become red.

Key insight: Blue pixels on a bar encode its danger radius — more blue means a wider no-go zone the path must respect.
</description>

<reference_puzzle index="11" source="eval" puzzle_id="7666fa5d" lines="12-26">
<description>
Fill the parallelogram-shaped region between parallel anti-diagonal line segments with red.

Input structure:
- bg = most common color.
- One non-bg color forms short diagonal segments, each lying on a single anti-diagonal (r+c = const).
- Segments come in parallel pairs bounding a region to be filled.

Rule:
- For each main-diagonal axis (r−c = const), collect anti-diagonal segments whose span covers that axis.
- Sort those segments by their anti-diagonal index (r+c).
- Fill all bg cells lying strictly between consecutive segment pairs along that axis with red (color 2).

Key insight: The two diagonal directions decouple — segments sit on anti-diagonals, and filling sweeps along main diagonals between them, forming parallelogram regions.
</description>
</reference_puzzle>

<description>
Move a triangular sprite across the grid, consuming aligned marker pixels.

Input structure:
- Frames concatenated horizontally with solid divider columns; bg is most common color.
- Each frame: a triangular arrow-shaped sprite and scattered single-pixel markers, all one color.

Rule:
- Start from the rightmost frame.
- At each step, exactly one cardinal direction from the head has reachable
  markers (on a half-line: same row or column); the sprite advances to the
  nearest one.
- On reaching a marker, consume it; sprite re-faces that direction.
- Repeat until no markers remain; output the final sprite position on a clean bg.

Key insight: The triangle's tip encodes facing direction, which sets marker-consumption priority.

<description>
Extract the top-left or top-right 2x2 corner crop selected by the top edge clue.

Input structure
- The input is a square color grid whose answer is one of the two 2x2 crops
  touching the top edge and a grid corner.
- A repeated color on the top row, when present, acts as the primary side cue.
- Otherwise, the two top corner colors have different whole-grid frequencies.

Rule
- If a top-row color repeats, find the repeated top-row occurrence nearest to
  a left or right edge and take the 2x2 top-corner crop on that side.
- If the top row has no repeated color, compare the whole-grid frequencies of
  the two top-corner colors and take the side whose corner color is rarer.

Key insight
- The task is not asking for an arbitrary 2x2 search: the decisive information
  is on the top edge, and it selects which top corner crop to keep.
</description>

<description>
Crop out the grid half containing the two aligned marker pixels, then erase the markers to background.

Input structure
- The grid has a dominant background color and one non-background marker color
  that appears as exactly two cells.
- The marker cells are aligned either in one row or one column and both lie
  in the same half along that direction.

Rule
- If the markers share a row, keep the left or right half whose columns
  contain them.
- If the markers share a column, keep the top or bottom half whose rows
  contain them.
- In the kept half, recolor the marker pixels to the background color.

- The two marker pixels are not part of the picture; their alignment and
  half-position identify which half of the doubled canvas is the answer.

<description>
Draw a polyline by decoding 3x3 glyph instructions read across a divided grid.

Input structure:
- Left of a full-height vertical divider: 3x3 colored glyphs on bg.

Rule:
- Remove the divider; shift the gray start into the left canvas.
- Read glyphs in column-major order (left→right, top→bottom).
- Each glyph's shape (pixel area) encodes a segment: area 4 → down 2, area 5 → right 3, area 6 → left 4, area 7 → left 2.

Key insight: Glyph pixel count (area) uniquely determines both segment length and direction — the shape itself is the instruction.

注意事项：
1. 任务不应依赖大量先验知识或复杂计算，而应依赖对模式、结构与空间关系的直觉推理。
2. 若涉及计算，只允许使用简单的计数、排序与数量比较；不允许使用复杂的数学公式或运算。
3. 问题必须能从少量示例中推断出来，但不代表题目一定简单。设计时需从人类解题者的视觉角度思考：规则涉及的所有关键要素（对象、关系、属性）必须在网格上肉眼可见，不应依赖人眼无法直接观察到的隐含信息或需要推算的隐藏属性。规则可以很难发现，难到反复对比所有样例仍需要深度思考才能归纳出来，但一旦发现后必须能用来解释所有样例且产出唯一确定的输出。
4. 生成的问题描述旨在促进后续用于创建题目网格的代码生成。因此，对输入/输出网格的描述以及规则总结必须极其详细；语言风格应易于理解；对于特定对象（如箭头），必须在描述中明确其绘制方式。
5. 描述谜题时避免使用 0 或 1 这类颜色编号；应使用颜色名称，例如 “red”。可用颜色如下：Black、Blue、Red、Green、Yellow、Grey、Magenta、Orange、Azure、Maroon。
6. 五个新题的核心推理机制应明显不同——即从观察样例到归纳规则的认知路径不同，而不仅仅是场景、对象或颜色不同。
7.  题目难度应来自机制的隐蔽性而非步骤数。关键信息难以准确定位，需要反复对比尝试不同的角度才能发现真正的规则。


生成后对生成的题执行以下四项审查（审查内容不需要保存到文件）：
1. 原创性
  新题不可与上述20道参考题相同或者是类似，不可只是替换了颜色、方向、对象类型、维度等表层要素。判断标准：新题的核心推理路径（解题者从样例中归纳规则的认知过程）应与任何一道参考题不同。
2. 确定性
  规则完全确定，同一输入只能产生唯一输出，每一步操作都有明确定义。
3. 可实现性
  所有必要细节都已给出——对象如何生成、如何识别、如何变换、边界情况如何处理，读完描述后可以直接写代码。
4. ARC 合规性
  网格尺寸合理（≤30×30），使用颜色不超过 10 种，规则复杂度适中，人类能从 3-5个样例中发现规律。
